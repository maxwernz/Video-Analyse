# Handoff: Prototype B -- QML / Qt Quick presentation layer

You are building **one of two competing throwaway prototypes**. Prototype A is a
redesigned Qt Widgets interface, built in parallel by another agent from
`HANDOFF-prototype-a-widgets.md`. You are not building the production migration.

**QML is a candidate, not a foregone conclusion. QML alone does not create good
design.** Your job is to prove Qt Quick both visually and technically, honestly
enough that it could lose.

## Read first

1. `docs/design/desktop-ux.md` -- the direction, the fairness rules, the decision
   criteria and the kill conditions. Authoritative.
2. `docs/design/visual-tokens.md` -- **frozen**. Colours, type, metrics, icons and
   the canonical fixture. Consume exactly; do not tune, do not edit this file.
3. `docs/design/references/` -- Catapult (primary), In-Play (secondary),
   TactiCode. Visual character only, never features.
4. `docs/design/current-ui/` -- what is being replaced, with the worst areas
   marked. Read `*-marked.png` and make sure you are not reproducing any of it.
5. `docs/adr/0007-branded-dark-visual-system.md` and
   `docs/adr/0006-one-compact-timeline-as-the-sole-seek-surface.md`.

## Build in

`prototype/presentation_b_qml/` and nowhere else.

## What to build

Two screens, at a designed minimum of 1440x900:

1. **Normal workspace** -- 44px working toolbar (no product wordmark), left
   sidebar with Clips/Videos as a segmented control, Clip list grouped by
   Category with 32px rows, near-black video stage, the 60px timeline, the 56px
   transport.
2. **Clip-editing state** -- large paused frame left, 360px sectioned form right,
   monospace scrub-linked boundary fields with a live duration readout, accent
   primary and quiet secondary actions.

Also render the **empty state** if time allows: the designed drop target, not a
black rectangle.

The timeline is the highest-value surface and the sharpest technical test. Build
it properly: a 16px ruler with adaptive labelled minute marks, a 44px track,
Clip ranges as filled bars at 40% alpha with a 1px full-colour top edge, a 2px
accent playhead with a grabbable handle, hover cursor line and timecode tooltip.
Implement the full interaction grammar from ADR 0006 -- drag anywhere scrubs,
click a range selects without seeking, double-click seeks to the Clip start.

## Architectural constraint -- the point of the exercise

The existing Python must survive the migration. Retain `analysis/`,
`application_workflow.py` and `playback/` as-is and drive QML from Python:

- Expose state to QML through `QObject` properties, signals and slots, or Qt
  models -- your call, but justify it.
- **Application logic stays in Python.** JavaScript in QML is for presentation
  only. If you find yourself moving workflow or model rules into JS, stop and
  record it: that is one of B's named kill conditions.
- Video must render into the Qt Quick scene graph. Prove it with a real file, not
  a placeholder rectangle. `playback/fake.py` is fine for headless captures but
  is **not** sufficient evidence for the video-surface question.

## Do not

- Modify anything outside `prototype/presentation_b_qml/`, except appending your
  findings to your own README.
- Touch `prototype/presentation_a_widgets/` or read it before you have finished
  your own build. Parallel isolation is what makes the comparison fair.
- Edit `docs/design/visual-tokens.md`. If a value is genuinely wrong, note it in
  your findings and use it anyway.
- Adopt any excluded reference feature: annotation tools, tagging button grids,
  stacked lane timelines, event registry tables, sharing or multi-user chrome.

## Deliverables

1. A runnable prototype: `README.md` with the exact run command.
2. Screenshots at 1440x900 in `prototype/presentation_b_qml/screenshots/`, one
   per screen, same fixture as the spec.
3. `FINDINGS.md` reporting **against the five decision criteria** in
   `desktop-ux.md`: visual result, timeline rendering and interaction quality,
   playback and scrub performance, packaging on macOS **and** Windows, and how
   much Python the presentation layer needs plus how testable it stays.
4. An explicit verdict on each **kill condition** for B: did video rendering or
   packaging regress on either platform; did logic leak into JavaScript.
5. An honest list of what you could not make work. A prototype that hides its
   failures is worthless for this decision.

Report packaging as a real observation -- attempt a build with the existing
`scripts/build_macos.sh` path and say what breaks -- not as an estimate.
